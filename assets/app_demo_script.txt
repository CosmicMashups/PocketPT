Now that we are done presenting the manuscript, let us now move on with the prototype. Since PocketPT is a mobile application, we will be using smartphones for the test run. 

First of all, there are two options for where the user will begin. If the user has already logged in, they will be redirected to the Dashboard Page. If not, users will start by creating an account, during which they are required to provide their name, email address, and password, as well as to agree to the Terms of Service (where it is stated that the rehabilitation plan generated shall only serve as advice, and should not replace professional advice from physical therapists) and Privacy Policy (where it is stated that details of the users shall only be used for rehabilitation purposes and shall not be used by third-party applications not affiliated with the application). The user's name will be used in generating progress reports, while the email and password will serve as login credentials for future sessions. Additionally, PocketPT supports Google Sign-In for convenient authentication, and users can also access the application in guest mode for exploring features without creating an account.

Before finalizing the registration, users must undergo a comprehensive guided assessment first. This step is critical, as it collects key information including the rehabilitation goal, the muscle region they wish to focus on, descriptions of any pain experienced—such as its level, type, and duration—and details about recent or past injuries. The assessment workflow consists of multiple stages: first, a preliminary assessment where users set their rehabilitation goals and understand the assessment process; then, a core assessment where they evaluate pain characteristics, select specific muscle groups, and provide injury history; followed by a camera-based assessment for Range of Motion evaluation.

For evaluating pain and range of motion, PocketPT incorporates a process that physical therapists do in a traditional setting called the Active Range of Motion (AROM) method, enhanced by AI-powered pose estimation and pain recognition. The system uses a custom YOLO11s-pose model for real-time pose detection, tracking 17 key body landmarks in COCO format to analyze movement patterns. During the camera assessment, users perform specific movements based on their selected muscle group, and the system automatically detects the appropriate ROM assessment algorithm through a modular assessment service architecture—supporting assessments for deltoids, biceps, triceps, quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, gluteals, chest, and trunk muscles including abdominals, obliques, lower back, and multifidus. Each muscle group has its own specialized assessment module that calculates joint angles, measures displacement, and evaluates range of motion using clinically validated thresholds. The system measures joint angles, detects compensatory movements such as pelvic tilt or torso lean, and provides real-time color-coded feedback indicating mobility levels as severe, moderate, or good. All assessment logic is fully integrated within the camera assessment page, processing frames in real-time at optimized frame rates to ensure smooth performance on mobile devices. For users who prioritize privacy or cannot use the camera, there is also an option to manually input a pain level on a scale from 1 to 10, or to upload photos or videos for analysis.

Additionally, PocketPT features advanced pain recognition using PyTorch-based facial pain detection models that can identify low, moderate, or severe pain levels in real-time during assessments. This AI-powered pain recognition works alongside the pose estimation to provide comprehensive evaluation of the user's condition. The system processes facial expressions at a controlled frame rate to balance accuracy and performance, providing immediate visual feedback through color-coded overlays and intervention dialogs when moderate or severe pain is detected.

For instance, if my focus is on relieving the pain in my deltoids, which I've been experiencing for the past month, and I would describe the pain as muscle soreness without any prior injuries, I would select the options for Alleviate Pain, then Upper Body, followed by Deltoids. Next, I would perform the Range of Motion test. For the deltoids, this involves carefully raising the arms. After the system processes this using pose detection, it detects the pain level and ROM limitations. Among the types of pain, the most accurate description would be Muscle Pain, which includes sore muscles. Then, I would specify that the pain has been present for about three weeks, with no past conditions influencing the injury. After reviewing the details provided, I can proceed by clicking Start Plan to generate a rehabilitation plan that includes exercises and treatments.

Based on the collected data, PocketPT automatically generates a personalized rehabilitation plan using a curated dataset verified by licensed physical therapists. This plan is further refined by a rule-based decision tree algorithm that ensures the exercises prescribed are both safe and effective. The system also generates complementary treatment recommendations, which may include modalities such as heat therapy, cold therapy, or other interventions appropriate for the user's condition. Upon completion of the assessment, the registration is finalized, granting users access to the full set of application features.

Once registered, users can log in and access various features including the Dashboard, Exercises Library (also called the Plan page), Record, Reports, and Profile pages. After logging in, users are directed to the Dashboard, which serves as the central hub for monitoring their rehabilitation progress. The Dashboard displays the user's current exercise, notifications about upcoming sessions or important updates, exercise streak tracking consecutive days of activity, total completed exercises, and total time spent on rehabilitation. Additionally, users can approve therapist requests to view their progress and optionally add exercises outside of their prescribed plan. They can also begin or resume their rehabilitation using the Start/Resume button, or access daily assessment features for ongoing monitoring of their condition.

The Plan page, also known as the Exercise Library, provides users with comprehensive exercise management capabilities. Users can replace specific exercises, delete existing ones, and add new exercises from the application's extensive database. The system also supports custom exercise creation, allowing users to define their own exercises with complete metadata including name, description, muscle group, pain level, functional goal, sets, repetitions, and media resources. Each exercise card includes a representative image, the targeted muscle group, and the acceptable pain threshold. When expanded, the card reveals comprehensive details such as step-by-step instructions, associated rehabilitation goals, and the recommended number of sets and repetitions. This feature empowers users to customize their rehabilitation experience by exploring and incorporating exercises beyond those included in their personalized program, making the system adaptable to individual needs and therapist recommendations.

To track progress, PocketPT includes a dedicated Record feature with a comprehensive exercise recording workflow. Users begin at the Pre-Record page, which shows a live camera view, the upcoming exercise, its sequence number, required sets and repetitions, and the targeted muscle group. Before starting, users have the option to engage in a warm-up stretching routine tailored to their selected muscle group, which helps prevent injury and prepares the body for exercise. Once the recording starts, users can view the exercise duration through a real-time stopwatch, pause the timer, and switch between exercises in their plan. During recording, the system provides real-time pose detection with skeleton overlay visualization, form analysis to ensure proper exercise execution, and AI-powered pain recognition that monitors facial expressions for signs of discomfort. If moderate or severe pain is detected, the system provides appropriate warnings and recommendations. After completing the final activity, users are directed to a Confirm Progress page where they can choose to save or discard their session data. Following exercise completion, users also have the option to perform a cooldown stretching routine to aid recovery and reduce muscle soreness.

The Reports page functions as a comprehensive log of a user's rehabilitation journey. It compiles data from the initial assessment and each recorded session, including exercise dates, sets, repetitions, and durations. The page features an interactive exercise calendar that visualizes workout history, pain level trend charts showing progress over time, and detailed expansion panels for each rehabilitation plan with complete exercise history. Users may export this data as a professionally formatted PDF that uses medically appropriate terminology, making it ideal for consultations with healthcare professionals. The PDF includes comprehensive sections covering user information, assessment summary, exercise history, pain tracking, and progress analytics, all formatted for clinical documentation standards.

In the Profile page, users can update personal details such as their name, email, and password. The system includes secure password change functionality with validation to ensure strong passwords. Users can also adjust app settings including daily reminders and exercise reminders through toggle switches. The profile page provides access to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, which emphasize that PocketPT is intended for informational use only. The development team maintains a strong commitment to data privacy, user confidentiality, and ethical research practices, ensuring that all submitted information—whether from users or therapists—is securely handled and protected. The application implements offline-first architecture with dual storage using Hive for local persistence and Firebase for cloud synchronization, ensuring data is always accessible even without internet connectivity and automatically synced when connection is restored.

PocketPT also includes several advanced features that enhance the rehabilitation experience. The application features guided tutorials that help new users understand how to navigate and use various features effectively. There is a daily assessment feature that allows users to perform ongoing ROM evaluations and pain tracking to monitor their progress between formal exercise sessions. The system includes a pose estimation demo that showcases the AI capabilities for educational purposes. Additionally, the application supports multiple assessment modes for different muscle groups, with automatic algorithm selection based on the user's selected focus area, eliminating the need for manual configuration.

Overall, PocketPT provides a structured, adaptive, and AI-enhanced rehabilitation experience that empowers users to manage their physical recovery efficiently and safely from their mobile devices. By combining advanced machine learning models including a custom YOLO11s-pose model for pose detection, PyTorch-based pain recognition, comprehensive ROM assessment algorithms implemented through a modular service architecture, and clinically informed decision-making through rule-based plan generation, the application offers a viable solution for users seeking personalized rehabilitation support, especially in settings where access to in-person therapy may be limited. The system's offline-first design, comprehensive data tracking, professional reporting capabilities, and extensive exercise library make it a complete rehabilitation companion that bridges the gap between clinical care and home-based recovery.

The technical implementation of the assessment system is fully integrated within the application. The camera assessment page processes real-time video input through a sophisticated pipeline: it first detects body pose using the custom YOLO11s-pose model, which tracks 17 key body landmarks in COCO format (including nose, eyes, ears, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, and ankles). These landmarks are normalized to a 0.0-1.0 coordinate range and then passed to the modular assessment service, which automatically routes to the appropriate muscle-specific assessment algorithm based on the user's selected focus area.

The heart of the ROM assessment system uses specialized algorithms for each muscle group, all implemented as modular services within the application. For example, during calf assessment, the system measures the horizontal displacement between the knee and ankle, normalizing this movement relative to the hip-ankle height. It then classifies the results using clinically validated thresholds: if the normalized displacement is less than 0.15, it's categorized as severe; if it's between 0.15 and 0.30, it's moderate; and if it's greater than or equal to 0.30, it's considered good. The hamstring assessment measures the angle formed by the leg when raised straight, using thresholds where angles less than 140° indicate low pain (good flexibility), angles between 140° and 160° indicate moderate limitation, and angles at or above 180° indicate severe limitation. For upper body assessments like triceps, the system measures extension angles with thresholds of less than 90° for severe, between 90° and 135° for moderate, and greater than or equal to 135° for good range. Shoulder assessments use similar threshold-based classification with angles less than 90° for severe, between 90° and 110° for moderate, and up to 150° for low pain levels.

Additionally, the system looks for compensatory movements, such as pelvic tilting or torso lean, which might occur if the user is using other body parts to complete the movement. If the system detects that the vertical difference in hip position is more than 5% of the torso height, it flags this as a potential compensation issue. The system also monitors shoulder elevation and other compensation patterns to ensure accurate ROM measurements. To make it easy for the user to understand the results, the system uses a color-coded feedback system (red for severe, orange for moderate, and green for good), displayed in real-time on the camera preview, so users can immediately see how well they're doing during the tests. The system features automatic algorithm selection that dynamically routes to the appropriate assessment module based on the user's selected muscle group, eliminating the need for manual configuration. The modular architecture ensures that each assessment type has its own optimized algorithm with clinically validated thresholds, while maintaining a consistent interface through the unified AssessmentService.

For pain recognition, the system integrates a PyTorch-based facial pain detection model that analyzes facial expressions in real-time during assessments and exercise recording. The model classifies pain into three categories: low, moderate, and severe, and processes frames at a controlled rate of approximately 5 frames per second to balance accuracy and performance. During assessments, this pain recognition works alongside ROM evaluation to provide a comprehensive picture of the user's condition, with results displayed in real-time through color-coded overlays. During exercise recording, the system continuously monitors for pain and provides progressive interventions: low pain is ignored as normal, moderate pain triggers informational banners with rest recommendations that can be dismissed, and severe pain displays safety dialogs with options to continue or rest, with cooldown protection to prevent dialog spam.

The system's data architecture implements a dual-storage approach using Hive for local persistence and Firebase for cloud synchronization. This offline-first design ensures that all critical data, including user profiles, assessment results, exercise plans, exercise history, pain tracking, and progress data, is stored locally and remains accessible even without internet connectivity. When network connectivity is restored, the system automatically synchronizes data between local and cloud storage, with conflict resolution to handle concurrent modifications. This architecture supports 13 different Hive models covering all aspects of user data, ensuring comprehensive data persistence and reliability.

To ensure the system's accuracy and clinical relevance, the ROM assessment algorithms are based on established physical therapy standards and threshold values validated against clinical measurements. The pose detection system uses a custom YOLO11s-pose model that provides reliable landmark detection across different body types and lighting conditions. The pain recognition model has been trained on clinical pain expression datasets to ensure accurate classification. The system processes real-time camera input efficiently, with frame processing throttled to approximately 120-150 millisecond intervals to balance responsiveness and performance, ensuring smooth operation on mobile devices while maintaining assessment accuracy.

The research provides valuable data on the effectiveness of vision-based assessments compared to traditional methods, offering insights into how these new technologies can improve rehabilitation and mobility assessments. The system's modular architecture allows for easy extension to assess additional joints and muscle groups beyond the current implementation, with new assessment modules following the same interface pattern. The integrated nature of the system means that all processing happens in real-time on the device, providing immediate feedback to users without requiring external services or network connectivity. In the future, the system could be enhanced further by integrating machine learning to personalize the thresholds for each user based on their baseline measurements, or to provide more sophisticated exercise form analysis. Ultimately, the system aims to standardize range of motion evaluations, reduce human error, and make remote physiotherapy monitoring more accessible, particularly for users who might not be able to visit a clinic regularly.
